Restatement of the Controlling Task
1. Objective. Select exactly one debris-remediation concept direction with a credible 2026-era technical path, a defensible cost-performance advantage, and a clear value wedge — by generating 4–6 candidates and comparing them ruthlessly against six baseline categories of existing ADR/remediation. If no candidate has a credible advantage, say so plainly and present a narrower, more defensible fallback with the same rigor.

2. Hard constraints. No magical propulsion, autonomy, sensing, free launch, unlimited compute, or unconstrained legal permission. Real programs labeled with as-of dates and confidence; web search was available and was used — statuses below are web-verified as of 2026-07-05 unless labeled training-data. Value metric must not default to kg removed. RL neither banned nor forced — justified either way. poliastro is archived and is not used. Tone: skeptical, engineering-focused, no hype.

3. Expected output. Eight sections: Competitive Landscape; Candidate Concepts; Scoring Matrix; Selected Concept (or explicit no-winner + fallback); Kill Criteria; Value Thesis; Simulation Thesis; Final One-Paragraph Concept Brief.

1. Competitive Landscape
(All program statuses web-verified as of 2026-07-05 unless noted; confidence labels given per program.)

1.1 One-servicer-one-target robotic capture + controlled reentry.
Target class: large intact unprepared LEO derelicts (90 kg–8 t). Maturity: pre-first-demonstration after 13 years and ~$400M of programs. As of 2026-07-05, no one has ever robotically captured an unprepared object in LEO (Astroscale ADRAS-J completed inspection-only ops at 15 m standoff, March 2026 — high confidence; one autonomous-approach abort en route). Caveat from cross-check: China's Shijian-21 towed the defunct Beidou-2 G2 to a super-graveyard orbit in Jan 2022 (high confidence) — so "never by anyone" holds only for LEO/tumbling/Western programs. Pipeline: ADRAS-J2 (¥13.2B ≈ $85–90M for one 3-t stage; launch JFY2027; contract high confidence), UK ADR (£75.6M for two smallsats, launch end-2028, award outcome unverified — medium confidence), ClearSpace-1 (>€100M for one 94-kg satellite, retargeted to PROBA-1 after its original target was itself struck by debris in 2023; slipped to 2028–29). ESA's e.Deorbit (€300M for Envisat) died at ministerial level in 2018. Cost driver: a bespoke, expended servicer per target. Scalability: arithmetically nil — the top-50 priority list at demonstrated prices is ~$4.5B/decade against ~$0.4B of total global committed spend 2020–2026. Regulatory: OST Art. VIII means the buyer's own objects only; no salvage doctrine exists. Safety: capture of a tumbling aged object can cause the fragmentation it prevents. Buyers: three governments (JAXA, ESA, UKSA), all buying national-flag missions. Beatability: easy on paper economics, nearly impossible on procurement politics — the moat is sovereignty, not engineering.

1.2 Multi-target removal of prepared satellites (docking plates).
Target class: pre-plated cooperative constellation satellites — in practice, 568 plated OneWeb satellites plus a 100+ plate Airbus order (high confidence). Maturity split: interface TRL 9; capture demonstrated once, teleoperated, on a 17-kg purpose-built client (ELSA-d, 2021 — the autonomous attempt was aborted and never retried); service TRL ~6. ELSA-M slipped to NET FY2028 on Isar Spectrum, a rocket with a 0-for-1 record (launch contract 2026-03-13, high confidence). Price signal: $8–13M per removal vs ~$1M satellite build cost and near-free self-deorbit. Scalability cap: Starlink, Kuiper, and Chinese constellations all declined plates — >90% of the future dead-satellite population is excluded by design. Buyer: essentially one financially strained customer (Eutelsat OneWeb) plus agency subsidy. Beatability: the honest competitor is higher-reliability self-disposal, which shrinks this market toward a rounding error.

1.3 Reusable servicer + expendable shepherd / deorbit kits.
GEO dock-tow-release is the category's only real success (Northrop MEV-1 completed a full 5-year service cycle and undocked April 2025 — high confidence; note MEV grapples the unprepared LAE nozzle, cooperative but not "plated"). LEO is TRL 6–7 pretending to be 9: Starfish Space has ~$160M in disclosed government awards and zero completed LEO dockings in two attempts over three years (Otter Pup 1 propulsion failure; Otter Pup 2 still not docked 12 months after launch — high confidence). The pivotal datapoint: SDA paid $52.5M for one contracted LEO deorbit with undisclosed option pricing (Jan 2026, high confidence); NASA pays $15M for multi-target inspection (SSPICY, launch late 2026). Robotic kit installation (MRV/MEP analog) has never flown. Passive kits (Terminator Tape, ADEO) are flight-proven but only pre-installed. Also missing from most surveys: active electrodynamic tether kits (E.T.PACK-F, first flight demo manifested 2026 — medium confidence) and SpaceWERX Orbital Prime ($121M across 175 small contracts — the largest US ADR-adjacent funding line; medium-high confidence). Scalability: months-per-target electric phasing, plane changes prohibitive; single digits per vehicle. Beatability: real, but only by out-flying, not out-promising.

1.4 Non-contact nudging / just-in-time collision avoidance (JCA).
TRL 2–4 across the entire category; zero end-to-end flight demonstrations anywhere (high confidence). Physics bifurcates the category: photon pressure (6.7 N/GW on-target) yields ~1.6e-8 m/s² on an 8-t stage — JCA-scale mm/s–cm/s nudges at best, never deorbit; pulsed ablation flips to cm-class fragments. NASA LightForce study result (4 sites, 20 kW, ~85% conjunction mitigation coverage) remains unfunded paper since ~2016. EOS Space Systems claims (unverified independently) photon-pressure perturbation; EX-Fusion×EOS MOU has produced no on-sky milestone (medium confidence); Orbital Lasers ($20M Series A, March 2026, high confidence) targets space-based ablation detumble, demo 2027. CNES SpaceBlower (suborbital particle cloud JCA) is stalled pre-Phase-A. NASA OTPS CBA rates laser JCA best-case ~300:1 benefit/cost — the strongest unclaimed procurement argument in the field. Regulatory: the worst position of any category — operationally indistinguishable from counterspace dazzling; Russia/China have formally objected at COPUOS. Buyers: none yet; debris-on-debris risk is a textbook public good. Beatability: no incumbent moat, but the barrier is trust (who gets licensed to lase orbital objects), not hardware.

1.5 Small-debris (1 mm–10 cm) remediation.
The lethal-non-trackable regime (145k–425k objects at 1–10 cm; population models diverge 3×). TRL 2–4; zero removals ever demonstrated. NASA OTPS Phase 1/2 (2023/2024, both read and verified — high confidence) found ground/space laser removal of cm-class debris to be among the most cost-effective remediation modes in the best case (~100:1 BCR, $300–6,000/object marginal) — but drag-rule compliance and 3-mm shielding reach up to 1000:1, the pessimistic laser case is net-negative, rep-rate coupling on realistic tumbling multi-material fragments is undemonstrated, and a weak nudge is worse than none (perigee-lowering without reentry increases ISS-crossing flux). Sweepers/foam/dust: NASA costed tungsten dust at $408–816M with a speculative mechanism; cm-class sweepers "too high to be relevant." Tracking floor: Space Fence detects ~5 cm but the maintained catalog stays ≥10 cm. Buyers: none — the purest public good in the field. Beatability: technologically wide open, commercially nearly hopeless.

1.6 Pure SSA / STM.
The only mature, operational, revenue-generating category — and the referee that disciplines all the others. TraCSS survived a near-termination in the FY26 budget cycle and is heading to production in 2026 (~$55M/yr for ~80% of active satellites screened — high confidence); LeoLabs closed 2025 with >$60M in awards, growth driven 186% YoY by defense, not operators; verified commercial price anchor: $2,500/mo/satellite. EU SST: 19 member states, free services (April 2026, high confidence). The structural facts: conjunction data is free at point of use (space-track, TraCSS, EU SST), so operator willingness-to-pay is thin; SSA reduces risk only for maneuverable spacecraft; dead-on-dead conjunctions — the Kessler feedstock — are untouched. NASA OTPS: tracking+avoidance generally beats large-object removal per dollar on operational timescales. Beatability: near-unbeatable as risk-reduction-per-dollar; the two exploitable seams are (a) dead-on-dead conjunctions and (b) covariance quality on derelicts, where TLE-class uncertainty (~0.5–2 km along-track) makes most red alerts false alarms.

Cross-cutting economics (the referee's numbers). Real observed prices: $85M/object ($28M/t) for ADRAS-J2; ~$47M/object UK; >€1M/kg for ClearSpace-1. NASA Phase 1 monetized the benefit of removing the top-50 derelicts at ~$3.5M/yr — a 1–2 order-of-magnitude cost/benefit mismatch on any commercial horizon. Total identifiable global remediation procurement 2020–2028: ~$300–400M, enough for ~4 of ~2,000 massive derelicts. The four massive-derelict clusters (775/850/975/1500 km, ~800,000 kg) are overwhelmingly Russian-registered and legally frozen (OST Art. VIII; no consent forthcoming). No deposit/bounty/fee demand mechanism exists in any jurisdiction. The office that wrote the field's economic backbone (NASA OTPS) was abolished March 2025. FCC enforcement precedent: one $150k fine.

D (Disposal Escrow), economics = kill despite top total: the FCC already operates NGSO surety bonds (47 CFR 25.165) adjudicated in-house against free USSF tracking data, forfeiting to Treasury — the mechanism exists and pays private verifiers exactly $0; a private oracle + private ADR fund is unlawful in the flagship jurisdiction without new primary legislation (Miscellaneous Receipts Act). 4. Selected Concept
No candidate survives the scoring with a credible advantage, and this must be stated plainly rather than papered over. The top scorer (Disposal Escrow, 25/40) carries a convincing economics kill that simulation cannot cure — its teeth require legislation that does not exist, and once narrowed to what is defensible it is a policy-mechanism-design study, not a remediation concept. Both 22-point hardware concepts aimed at where the risk actually lives (A, B) die on the same wall from two directions: the objects whose fragmentation dominates the statistical future of LEO are Russian and Chinese state property, and neither ion beams nor photons legally or politically touch them in 2026. E is dominated arithmetic. This is itself the report's most important finding: with 2026 technology, law, and buyers, no clean-sheet concept credibly beats the existing (weak) approaches at removing or stabilizing the risk-dominant objects.

Per the controlling spec, the narrower, more defensible fallback is therefore presented with full rigor:

Fallback: Covariance Compactor (narrowed) — government-anchored derelict characterization tours with consent-scoped retroreflector tagging
Mechanism. One or two ~24 kg (12–16U) inspector vehicles rideshare into the congested 600–1,000 km band and tour prioritized derelicts using differential-J2 nodal drift for plane matching (~0.05°/day per 50 km altitude offset; ~200 m/s budget per vehicle). Product 1 — standoff characterization (50 m–5 km, passive-abort-safe relative trajectories): resolved imaging, light curves → spin state and axis, mass-property refinement, MMOD/MLI condition, adapter integrity — sold as space-domain-awareness and covariance-truth data. Product 2 — observability modification: on consent-available (US/ESA/JAXA-registered) derelicts photometrically pre-confirmed as slow rotators (spin period ≳ 2 min), a dedicated rendezvous applies 2–4 adhesive corner-cube retroreflectors via guided applicator from <20 m with ODMSP-compliant (tethered/fast-decay) hardware, making the object trackable by the global SLR network: along-track covariance collapses from ~0.5–2 km to ~10–50 m, permanently. Product 3 — capture-grade dossiers on the buyer's own registered targets, de-risking the funded capture missions (ADRAS-J2, UK ADR, ClearSpace-1) whose inspection phases currently cost $15–25M each.
Target class. Consent-tiered: tagging on US/ESA/JAXA-registered derelict stages (Delta, Titan, Agena, H-2A heritage — dozens at 600–1,000 km); standoff-only characterization of the ~50 statistically-most-concerning massive derelicts, where observation requires notification, not consent (ADRAS-J set the precedent).
Intervention type. Inspection + risk-scoring, plus a defined "other": permanent physical modification of a target's observability class. It deliberately does not remove, deorbit, or nudge anything.
Required technologies. COTS smallsat bus + micro-propulsion; vision-based relative navigation to uncooperative tumblers (flown 2024–25); light-curve inversion; the tall pole is the tag — adhesion to 40-year-old degraded surfaces and optical usability on a rotating platform (unproven, but coupon-testable on the ground); SLR scheduling capacity (ILRS is oversubscribed — assume bought station time).
Buyer and price anchors (all real, all current). NASA paid Starfish $15M for multi-target defunct-satellite inspection (SSPICY — the direct comparable, verified); USSF/SSC buys commercial SSA data at $10M+ line items; JAXA/ESA/UKSA are already paying $15–25M-class inspection costs inside capture missions. Honest program price: $20–35M for 1–2 vehicles characterizing 20–40 objects (~$0.5–1M/object) and tagging 8–15 ($1.5–2.5M/object).
Why it survives where the others die. It is the only candidate with zero adversarial kills; every wound has a concrete narrowing that a simulation campaign can test; it is legally clean (the one structural advantage the landscape grants); it has the only demonstrated buyer class in the portfolio; and its differentiation survives the closest-competitor check — HEO's hosted-camera flybys are opportunistic km-standoff imaging with no ephemeris actuation, and no named program does or has announced retroreflector tagging of derelicts.
Why it may still fail (stated up front). It retires zero physical risk on its own — it is a force multiplier for removal and JCA campaigns that may never be funded; ground photometry and radar erode the inspection-only product every year; and the highest-value targets (SL-16s) are exactly the ones consent forecloses from tagging. Honest odds that a funded program emerges from a successful simulation campaign: ~25–35%.

5. Kill Criteria
Abandon the concept if any of the following trips during the 3–6 month simulation campaign (thresholds chosen to be actually trippable):

Value-of-information gate. In the Bayesian top-50 re-ranking experiment (mass ±50%, unknown spin state, TLE-class covariance priors vs simulated post-flyby posteriors), fewer than 2 of the top-10 removal-priority positions flip in ≥20% of Monte Carlo draws → the dossier product is dead; if gate 7 also trips, kill the whole concept.
Tour-economics gate. If the differential-J2 tour MILP over real current TLEs cannot schedule ≥8 standoff encounters per vehicle within 24 months inside a 200 m/s budget — costing every tagging target as a full rendezvous (~51 m/s per 50 km altitude-offset round trip plus proximity-ops margin), not a flyby — per-object cost exceeds ~$3M and the advantage over ADRAS-J-class inspection falls below 5× → kill.
Taggable-population gate. If the consent-available derelict set at 600–1,000 km that is simultaneously (a) confirmably slow-rotating (period > 2 min), (b) reachable within the Δv budget, and (c) >500 kg numbers fewer than 8 objects → drop the tagging line, re-evaluate as inspection-only.
Tag-efficacy gate. If coupon-parameterized contact/adhesion modeling (AO/UV-aged coatings) gives P(≥1 optically usable corner-cube per rendezvous) < 0.5, or SLR scheduling simulation (realistic ILRS oversubscription, weather, glint-window geometry) yields median along-track covariance improvement < 5× over radar-only → the observability wedge fails; drop tagging.
Erosion gate. If published ground-photometry/radar capability plus HEO-style hosted-camera flybys can deliver ≥80% of the dossier information content (spin axis to ~10°, period to ~1 s, gross structural condition) at <$100k/object → the dedicated-inspector premium is unjustified; kill.
Anchor-buyer arithmetic gate. If an anchor contract constructed strictly from observed comparables (SSPICY $15M/3 yr; USSF commercial-SSA line items) cannot cover ≥70% of the honestly-priced program at $0.5–1M per characterized and $1.5–2.5M per tagged object → no anchor closes in the 2026–2030 window; kill.
Red-alert value gate. If a 1-year Monte Carlo conjunction simulation shows the full consent-available tagged portfolio retires fewer than 5 red-alert reclassifications per year at realistic post-tag covariances → the operational-SSA value line is dead; concept survives only if gate 1 passes independently.
Debris-compliance gate. If no ODMSP-compliant applicator design keeps P(untracked released object with >25-yr lifetime per attempt) below 1e-2 in dispersion simulation → tagging is unlicensable for a US remediation-branded mission; drop tagging.

6. Value Thesis
The economic argument is deliberately not "we remove debris cheaper" — nobody in this report credibly does. It is: the marginal dollar in debris remediation currently buys decision quality, not mass removal, and decision quality is two orders of magnitude underpriced. The world's entire funded removal pipeline (~$300–400M, ~4 objects) is being aimed using a top-50 priority list built on ±50% mass uncertainty, guessed spin states, and km-class covariances; the inspection phase of every capture mission is being re-bought bespoke at $15–25M per target. This concept sells the same decision inputs at $0.5–1M per object (25–50× under the flown comparable) and, uniquely, makes the improvement permanent for tagged objects — a corner-cube costs nothing to operate and outlives every subscription. Chosen value metrics, in order: expected value of information (dollars of downstream removal/JCA spending correctly re-prioritized per dollar spent — a $25M campaign steering $500M+ of removal procurement is 20:1 leverage on other people's Δv); cost per high-risk object delivered with capture-grade characterization ($0.5–1M vs $15–25M flown); and cost per red-alert retired as the secondary, willingness-to-pay-testable metric (each tagged massive derelict generates 5–20 red alerts/yr at ~$10–50k of operator ops cost each, ~80% retired by tight covariance). Kilograms removed is zero by construction and is the wrong metric for every concept that survived adversarial review — the field's near-term value frontier is risk information and risk timing, not mass.

7. Simulation Thesis
The first simulator exists to answer one question: is better knowledge about massive derelicts worth dedicated hardware, or is it free-riding on sensors that already exist? Four decoupled studies, all pure-Python, no RL (there is no sequential-decision structure a MILP and Monte Carlo don't capture; every scheduling problem here is small combinatorial), no poliastro (archived; nothing here needs it):

Tour feasibility — sgp4 (pin sgp4==2.24) states for the real derelict population; numpy/scipy secular-J2 propagator; PuLP/scipy MILP tour scheduling under 200 m/s with rendezvous-class costing for tag targets. Pass/fail: gate 2.
Value of information — Bayesian re-ranking of the top-50 under current vs post-flyby knowledge, propagated through a NASA-standard-breakup-model-weighted collision-expectation model. The single output number is the fraction of Monte Carlo draws in which the top-10 removal ranking flips. Pass/fail: gate 1. This is the thesis experiment: if rankings are stable under today's ignorance, information has no purchase and the concept dies honestly.
Covariance business case — empirical covariance classes from differencing TLE predictions against precise ephemerides (numpy/pandas); 1-year conjunction Monte Carlo over ~40 candidate derelicts (scipy cKDTree screening, hand-rolled ~60-line Foster Pc — no maintained Pc package exists); count red-alert reclassifications under radar-only vs SLR-grade covariance. Pass/fail: gates 4 (second half) and 7.
Taggability census — skyfield/astropy pass geometry, photometric-confirmability model, and ILRS visibility/scheduling windows over the consent-available population. Pass/fail: gates 3 and 8.
Total stack: numpy, scipy, pandas, sgp4, skyfield, astropy, PuLP, pymsis for densities — everything reproducible in a requirements.txt with pinned versions, no heavyweight dependencies, 3–6 months of work for one engineer. The simulator does not model the tag's adhesion physics (ground coupon work, gate 4 first half) or the spacecraft bus — it models whether the mission is worth building at all.

8. Final One-Paragraph Concept Brief
Covariance Compactor (narrowed): a government-anchored derelict-intelligence mission for the congested 600–1,000 km LEO band, consisting of one or two ~24 kg smallsat inspectors that tour high-priority derelicts via differential-J2 plane drift (≥8 encounters per vehicle in 24 months within 200 m/s) and deliver three products: (1) standoff characterization (50 m–5 km) of the 50 statistically-most-concerning massive derelicts — spin state and axis, mass-property refinement, structural/MLI condition — sold as covariance-truth and space-domain-awareness data to USSF/SSC and NASA (direct price comparable: NASA's $15M SSPICY multi-target inspection award to Starfish Space); (2) permanent observability modification of consent-available US/ESA/JAXA-registered derelict stages that are photometrically confirmed slow rotators (spin period > 2 min), via 2–4 adhesive corner-cube retroreflectors applied by guided applicator from <20 m in a dedicated ODMSP-compliant rendezvous ($1.5–2.5M per tagged object), collapsing along-track covariance from ~0.5–2 km to 10–50 m via SLR and retiring false-red conjunction alerts; and (3) capture-grade feasibility dossiers on the buyer's own registered targets, de-risking funded removal missions (JAXA CRD2, UK ADR, ClearSpace-1) whose bespoke inspection phases currently cost $15–25M each. It removes nothing and nudges nothing — by design — because adversarial review of stronger-sounding alternatives (ion-beam cluster dispersal, ground-laser JCA, passivation, disposal escrow) showed each dies on Russian/Chinese ownership of the risk-dominant objects, invariant push-to-noise physics, or demand mechanisms requiring nonexistent legislation; this is the only concept that survived three-lens adversarial review with zero kill verdicts. Value metrics: expected value of information (removal dollars correctly re-prioritized per dollar spent; target 20:1), cost per capture-grade characterized object ($0.5–1M vs $15–25M flown comparable), cost per red-alert retired (secondary, WTP-testable).
